## Phase 2 — Surface scaffold implicit assumptions

Both [`entity-model.md`](../../ontology/entity-model.md) and [`lifecycle-semantics.md`](../../ontology/lifecycle-semantics.md) are at `Scaffold` status. Their prose was authored before Q2 was formally posed in [`ontology.md` §Open Questions](../../ontology/ontology.md) and before the [§2.5 redaction plan](../../charter/decision-log.md) (decision-log §0009) registered Q2 as a pre-redaction dependency. The question for this phase: do the scaffold sections explicitly defer, implicitly assume A, implicitly assume B, or contain a mixed pattern?

### `entity-model.md` Category III (lines 42–54) and §Open Modeling Questions (lines 62–71)

**Verdict: mixed, leaning B.**

- **Examples (lines 47–49)** present the four labels as lowercase descriptive phrases, each followed by a colon and a paraphrase of the inferential character: *"A behavioral cluster: 'these actors appear to be operated by the same entity.'"* The phrases are inferential characterizations, not proper type identifiers. Capitalization is reserved for the canonical labels when they appear in the Open Modeling Question (line 68); in the descriptive examples the labels read as kinds of inference, not as siblings of a type system. This phrasing is consistent with Candidate B's framing.
- **Structural properties (lines 51–54)** use uniform singular: *"Identity is probabilistic and may evolve..."*, *"Every claim about a hypothesis carries..."*, *"Lifecycle transitions are modeled as immutable events..."* The prose describes one entity with shared structure; it does not name per-subtype structural variation. This pattern is implicitly Candidate B — Candidate A's body prose would name the four sibling types and would distribute the structural properties across them or factor them into an abstract type.
- **Open Modeling Question 3 (line 68)** is the document's explicit deferral: *"Whether `BehavioralCluster`, `CoordinationRing`, `CampaignHypothesis`, and `AutomationGroup` are distinct types within Category III or are tags on a single type is undecided."* The deferral is procedurally clean, but its presence does not undo the B-flavor of the body prose.

Cost under Candidate A: rewriting the body prose of the Category III section. The structural properties bullets would expand to acknowledge the four sibling types or factor out shared structure to an abstract `Hypothesis`. The examples may keep their illustrative form but the relationship to the proper labels would need to be stated. Cost is non-trivial but bounded; the section is short.

Cost under Candidate B: minimal. The Open Modeling Question is closed; the body prose is already aligned. The deferral text in line 68 is replaced with a one-paragraph statement of resolution; no further rewrites of the section's substance are required.

### `lifecycle-semantics.md` Category III (lines 23–33), Promotion Mechanism (lines 35–46), and §Open Modeling Questions (lines 48–53)

**Verdict: implicitly assumes B.**

- **Lifecycle operations (lines 25–32)** are described with uniform-applicability language throughout. *"A hypothesis is formed when accumulated evidence crosses a threshold..."* (line 25); *"A hypothesis evolves through events..."* (line 26); *"Two hypotheses are recognized as describing the same underlying phenomenon and are combined into one"* (line 28, the merge operation). The merge operation is described without qualification by subtype — under Candidate A, cross-subtype merge becomes a non-trivial question requiring its own structural rule (see Phase 1 cell A2). The lifecycle-semantics.md prose pre-commits to merge as a type-uniform operation, which is structurally Candidate B.
- **Per Invariant 2.5 (line 33)** speaks of *"the current state of a hypothesis"* — singular, uniform. Under Candidate A, the analogue would be *"the current state of any record in Category III"* or *"of any of the four hypothesis types"*.
- **Promotion Mechanism (lines 35–46)** uses uniform singular throughout. Line 41 names *"every candidate promotion"* against criteria of maturity, breadth, and confidence — no subtype qualification. Line 44 names *"promoted hypotheses"* re-evaluated against fresh, independent evidence — no subtype qualification.
- **Open Modeling Question 1 (line 50)** binarizes the question into *"distinct lifecycle rules"* versus *"share a common lifecycle with category-specific parameters"*. The second option is the form Candidate B's lifecycle composition takes: one lifecycle with `kind`-keyed parameters. The framing of the question itself contains a B-preference: the alternative to A is named in terms that already structurally describe B. A clean deferral would have offered *"distinct types with distinct lifecycle rules, or a single type with optional per-`kind` parameters"* — separating the type-surface question from the parameter-distribution question. The current phrasing is not neutral.

Cost under Candidate A: rewriting most of the body prose of the Category III section and the Promotion Mechanism section. The lifecycle operations would need to be re-expressed polymorphically; merge would acquire a cross-subtype clause; Promotion Mechanism's criteria would be expressed per subtype or as a polymorphic structure over subtypes. The §Open Modeling Question 1 phrasing would also be revised to neutralize its B-preference. Cost is substantial; the affected prose is the bulk of the document.

Cost under Candidate B: low. The body prose is already aligned. The Open Modeling Question is closed by accepting the second option of its own binary. Minor rewording may be needed in Question 1's phrasing to acknowledge the answer is structurally what the question's second clause already named.

### Summary — implicit-assumption asymmetry

| Scaffold | Verdict | Cost under A | Cost under B |
|---|---|---|---|
| `entity-model.md` Category III | Mixed, leaning B | Non-trivial (body prose rewrite) | Minimal (deferral closure) |
| `lifecycle-semantics.md` Category III + Promotion Mechanism | Implicitly assumes B | Substantial (most prose rewritten) | Low (deferral closure + minor rewording) |

The asymmetry is structural, not a values judgment. The scaffolds were authored before Q2 was formally raised and they drifted in a B-favoring direction. Candidate A's apparent cost in extensibility and lifecycle dimensions (Phase 1 cells A2, A4) is partly the cost of *resolving* Q2 — work that becomes necessary once the question is on the table — and not the cost of Candidate A as such. Without this Phase 2 surfacing, the deliberation would weigh "A is expensive because the scaffolds disagree with it" without naming that the disagreement is itself an artifact of scaffold authorship preceding Q2's formal posing.

Implication for committee: Phase 1's evidence on dimensions 2 (lifecycle) and 4 (extensibility) should be re-read with the Phase 2 finding in mind. If the committee finds Candidate A's intrinsic structural-enforceability advantage (Phase 1 cell A5) decisive, the scaffold rework is a Q2-resolution cost, not a Candidate-A penalty.

### Most consequential epistemic finding across the six cells

The (B, `falsifiability-check`) cell's §1.3 partial pass and the (B, `epistemic-separator`) intra-category flattening risk are the same finding viewed through two skills: **under Candidate B, subtype-specific structural commitments live below the substrate's type-enforced surface.** They are real (a `BehavioralCluster` is genuinely different from a `CoordinationRing` in its inferential character), but they are not falsifiable at the type level; they are only falsifiable via runtime check or projection query. The Charter's structural-enforceability discipline ([§4 criterion 1, frozen v0.2](../../charter/constitutional-charter.md#4-constitutional-design-rule); [§4 falsifiability discipline](../../charter/constitutional-charter.md#4-constitutional-design-rule)) prefers structural enforcement over runtime enforcement. Candidate A passes this preference cleanly; Candidate B compromises on it in proportion to how much subtype-specific structure turns out to matter.

This finding is *conditional*: if the four subtypes turn out to share fully identical structure and operations, the Candidate B compromise is over a non-existent surface (there is nothing to enforce). Phase 2's scaffold reading is suggestive — the scaffolds currently assume uniform structure and operations, which is consistent with the four subtypes being structurally identical — but the scaffolds were written before Q2 was posed, so their uniformity is not committee-ratified evidence that the subtypes *are* identical, only that they *were written as if* identical.

### Calibration note for future Ontology RFCs

This is the precedent. For Q4 (next), Q1 (pre-§2.3), Q3 (pre-§2.6), Q5 (pre-§2.4):

- **Falsifiability is the dominant skill.** §1.3 (operationalization) tends to do most of the work on substrate-touching propositions. §1.1 (violation) and §1.2 (observation) are usually easier; §1.4 (non-circularity) tends to surface specific definitions, not whole propositions.
- **Epistemic-separator's most common finding is intra-category structural risk, not cross-category conflation.** Cross-category conflation is loud and easy to catch in early review (and the watchlists already filter most of it); intra-category flattening is the subtle failure mode that emerges when propositions resolve internal subdivision.
- **Ambiguity-reducer surfaces residual carry-forwards.** Each Ontology question's resolution typically carries one or two ambiguity flags that cannot be operationalized within the question itself — they become recorded open questions for a downstream resolution. This is the legitimate response 3 path; the alternative (silent operationalization) is the drift the skill exists to prevent.

The three skills are non-redundant: each surfaces a different shape of finding.

## Phase 4 — Comparison synthesis

Findings synthesized from Phase 1 (evidence matrix), Phase 2 (scaffold implicit assumptions), and Phase 3 (epistemic skills). Numbered in order of consequence for committee deliberation. Each finding is classified as **asymmetry** (one candidate clearly stronger by evidence-grounded argument), **apparent trade-off that resolves** (a Phase 1 trade-off whose appearance is reframed by Phase 2 or Phase 3), or **genuine trade-off** (a substantive difference that neither candidate clearly wins).

### Finding 1 — Asymmetry: structural enforceability of subtype-specific constraints favors Candidate A

Sources: Phase 1 cell A5 (constitutional implications); Phase 3 (B, `falsifiability-check`) §1.3 partial pass; Phase 3 (B, `epistemic-separator`) intra-category flattening risk.

Candidate A enforces subtype distinction in the type system. Subtype-specific structural constraints (e.g., a `BehavioralCluster` carries a `members` field; a `CoordinationRing` carries a `participants` field with cardinality ≥ 2) are expressible at the type level and reducible to type-system checks per [`falsifiability-check` §1.3](../../../.claude/skills/epistemic/falsifiability-check/SKILL.md).

Candidate B enforces only the existence and shape of the `kind` discriminator at the type level. Subtype-specific constraints migrate to runtime check or projection query. Per [Charter §4 criterion 1 (frozen v0.2)](../../charter/constitutional-charter.md#4-constitutional-design-rule), the project prefers structural enforcement over runtime enforcement: *"structurally enforceable — verifiable in schemas, types, or permitted operations, not merely in code review."*

The asymmetry is **conditional**: its weight depends on whether subtype-specific structural constraints actually exist. If the four subtypes share fully identical structure (no per-subtype fields, no per-subtype validation rules), the asymmetry collapses — Candidate B is compromising over a non-existent surface. Phase 2's scaffold reading is suggestive of uniform structure, but the scaffolds were authored before Q2 was formally posed, so their uniformity is not committee-ratified evidence that the subtypes *are* identical.

### Finding 2 — Apparent trade-off that resolves: scaffold rework under Candidate A is Q2-resolution cost, not Candidate-A intrinsic cost

Sources: Phase 2 (scaffold implicit assumption surfacing).

Phase 1 cells A2 (lifecycle) and A4 (extensibility) made Candidate A appear expensive: the lifecycle operations need polymorphic re-expression, the scaffolds' uniform-applicability prose needs rewriting, and the cost of adding a fifth subtype is heavier under A. Phase 2 surfaced that the scaffold uniformity is itself an artifact of authorship preceding Q2's formal posing — [`lifecycle-semantics.md`](../../ontology/lifecycle-semantics.md) implicitly assumes B, and its [Open Modeling Question 1 (line 50)](../../ontology/lifecycle-semantics.md) binarizes the question with a B-preference in its phrasing.

The implication: the scaffold rework cost under Candidate A is the cost of *resolving Q2*, not the intrinsic cost of choosing A. Without this surfacing, the deliberation weighs "A is expensive because the scaffolds disagree with it" — but the disagreement is an authorship-timing artifact, not a committee-ratified preference. The reframing does not eliminate Candidate A's lifecycle and extensibility costs; it relocates them from "Candidate-A penalty" to "Q2-resolution cost regardless of candidate".

### Finding 3 — Asymmetry: cross-subtype merge surface visibility favors Candidate A

Sources: Phase 1 cell A2 (lifecycle implications).

Under Candidate A, cross-subtype merge (e.g., a `BehavioralCluster` and a `CoordinationRing` merging into one entity) requires Q2's resolution to take a position: either permit cross-subtype merge as a typed transformation, or forbid it as a structurally invalid operation. The question becomes explicit and is structurally recorded in the resolution.

Under Candidate B, cross-`kind` merge is structurally permitted by default — the merge operation is uniform across `Hypothesis` instances regardless of `kind`. Whether cross-`kind` merge is semantically valid (does a "merged" entity have `kind = 'BehavioralCluster'`, `kind = 'CoordinationRing'`, or some third value?) is application-layer judgment, not structurally recorded.

The asymmetry favors Candidate A on falsifiability grounds: A's resolution explicitly records what cross-subtype operations are permitted; B's resolution leaves this question unanswered, and its answers accumulate in application code without RFC review. This is the same pattern Phase 2 surfaced — under B, an unresolved question lives below the substrate's structurally-enforced surface.

### Finding 4 — Asymmetry by analogy: intra-category flattening risk favors Candidate A

Source: Phase 3 (B, `epistemic-separator`).

Candidate B's construction (single record type with a `kind` discriminator field) is structurally analogous to [Charter §2.2's first forbidden anti-pattern](../../charter/constitutional-charter.md#22-epistemic-separation): *"unified assertion models. Defining a single generic record type with a 'kind' field distinguishing observation from inference from operational construct."*

The Charter scopes the anti-pattern to *cross-category* unification; Candidate B's `kind` is intra-Category-III, so it does not directly violate §2.2. But the structural intuition behind the anti-pattern — that a `kind` field substituted for a type distinction is a deferral of structural enforcement to runtime — carries over at the intra-category level with diminished but non-zero force. Phase 3's epistemic-separator finding documents the secondary risk: downstream consumers may read `kind` as an observational attribute rather than as an inferential commitment, flattening the epistemic character of the record.

The asymmetry is by analogy, not by direct constitutional rule. The committee may weight it more or less depending on whether it reads §2.2's anti-pattern as scope-specific (cross-category only) or as a deeper structural principle (which would scope to intra-category subdivision too).

### Finding 5 — Genuine trade-off: glossary and amendment ceremony

Sources: Phase 1 cell 4 (extensibility implications).

Candidate A imposes glossary entries for each subtype (each becomes a canonical term per [`vocabulary-discipline` §2](../../../.claude/skills/ontology/vocabulary-discipline/SKILL.md)) and a heavier gating ceremony for adding a fifth subtype (likely an `ontology-revision` RFC). Candidate B inherits less ceremony: a new `kind` value is a smaller surface, possibly without RFC review depending on the application contract.

Neither candidate is clearly stronger. The trade-off is between constitutional discipline (A: every constitutional term is in the glossary; every new subtype is RFC-reviewed) and operational simplicity (B: lightweight extension within an existing type). This is a values choice for the committee, not an evidence-grounded asymmetry.

### Finding 6 — Genuine trade-off: query and projection patterns

Sources: Phase 1 cell 3 (query and projection implications).

Candidate A favors typed queries against specific subtypes (narrowest, most efficient when subtype-specific queries dominate). Candidate B favors uniform queries against the single type (simplest when category-wide queries dominate).

Neither candidate is clearly stronger; the trade-off depends on which queries are expected to dominate the workload. Workload-shape predictions at this stage of the project are speculative; the trade-off resolves only once concrete query patterns are observed.

### Finding 7 — Carry-forward: identity-tier specification (residual ambiguity)

Sources: Phase 3 (A, `ambiguity-reducer`); Phase 3 (B, `ambiguity-reducer`).

Both candidates carry forward the identity-tier question ([`entity-model.md` Open Modeling Question 2](../../ontology/entity-model.md): `ActorRef` / `Identity` / `Cluster`). Under Candidate A, the question applies per subtype (four points where tier specification is needed); under Candidate B, the question applies at the single type level (one point).

Not a candidate-decisive finding — the question is open under either resolution. Recorded so that the carry-forward is not lost in the resolution prose.

### Summary statement

The evidence points toward **Candidate A**, conditional on the subtype-specific structural surface being non-trivial. Finding 1 (structural enforceability) is the strongest argument tied to Charter discipline (§4 criterion 1). Finding 2 (scaffold-rework reframing) removes the apparent intrinsic cost of A by relocating it to Q2-resolution cost. Findings 3 (cross-subtype merge visibility) and 4 (intra-category flattening risk) further favor A by surfacing-discipline and by-analogy arguments. The genuine trade-offs (Findings 5 and 6) are values choices that do not tip the balance.

The lean is not unconditional. If the committee surfaces evidence that the four subtypes share fully identical structure — no subtype-specific fields, no subtype-specific lifecycle parameters, no cross-subtype semantic distinctions — then Finding 1 collapses and Candidate B becomes preferable on operational-simplicity grounds. The next phase makes this conditional structure explicit.

## Phase 5 — Recommendation

The discussion phase recommends **Candidate A — Distinct types within Category III** as the resolution of Q2, conditional on the committee's assessment of subtype structural distinctness. The recommendation rests primarily on Finding 1 (structural enforceability of subtype-specific constraints aligns with Charter §4 criterion 1) and is supported by Finding 3 (cross-subtype merge surface visibility) and Finding 4 (intra-category flattening risk by analogy to §2.2's first forbidden anti-pattern). Finding 2 is load-bearing for the recommendation's *justification*, not its conclusion: without surfacing the scaffold-rework reframing, Candidate A would appear artificially expensive on Phase 1 dimensions 2 (lifecycle) and 4 (extensibility), and the deliberation would weight A's apparent cost against B's apparent simplicity; with the reframing, the scaffold rework is relocated to Q2-resolution cost regardless of which candidate wins, and the structural-enforceability asymmetry remains as the decisive evidence-grounded argument. The genuine trade-offs (Findings 5 and 6) are values choices the committee may resolve in either direction without contradicting the evidence; Finding 7 carries forward identically under either candidate. The lean is not unconditional — Finding 1's weight depends on whether subtype-specific structural commitments turn out to exist, which the discussion phase cannot definitively establish in the absence of committed type-system work — and the committee retains full latitude to resolve Q2 in B's favor if its assessment of that condition differs.

### What would reverse this recommendation

The recommendation flips to Candidate B if any of the following emerges:

- **Evidence that the four subtypes share fully identical structure.** No subtype-specific fields beyond the discriminator value; no subtype-specific validation rules; no cross-subtype semantic distinctions in lifecycle operations. If this is the committee's reading, Finding 1's structural-enforceability advantage collapses — Candidate B is no longer compromising on a real surface — and Findings 3 and 4 lose their force because there is nothing structurally distinct at the subtype level to surface or flatten.
- **A committee values-judgment that operational simplicity outweighs constitutional discipline.** Finding 5 is a genuine trade-off; if the committee weights operational simplicity (lightweight extension, smaller implementation surface, faster iteration) more heavily than constitutional discipline (every constitutional term in the glossary, every new subtype RFC-reviewed), Candidate B is the consistent resolution. This is a values-grounded choice, not an evidence-grounded one, and remains open per the recommendation's terms.
- **Evidence of upcoming Q2 churn.** If the committee anticipates that the four labels are themselves provisional and will be revisited (renamed, split, merged) under future Ontology work — for instance, if the [`entity-model.md` Open Modeling Question 3](../../ontology/entity-model.md) suggests the labels will mutate — Candidate B's lighter lock-in (per [`ontology-revision-q2-hypothesis-subtypes.md` §Migration and Backward Compatibility](../draft/ontology-revision-q2-hypothesis-subtypes.md)) is the prudent choice. Candidate A locks in four type definitions; Candidate B retains the option to mutate labels with smaller cost.

### Methodological observation

The discussion phase did not converge by the strength of any single finding; it converged by the *combination* of one direct asymmetry (Finding 1), one apparent-cost reframing (Finding 2), and two supporting asymmetries (Findings 3 and 4). This shape — one decisive finding supported by a reframing and several aligned secondary findings — is the precedent for how discussion-phase recommendations should be structured for future Ontology RFCs (Q4 next; then Q1, Q3, Q5). Convergence on a single finding alone would be evidence of insufficient deliberation; convergence by no finding at all is the legitimate "no recommendation" output.
